## Problem

The lab, its documents and its demo are behind a sign-in while they are being built.
That is the right default for work in progress and the wrong one for work that is
finished enough to be read. Publishing is not only removing a gate: the site promises
things about what it sends and keeps, search engines start indexing, and the demo starts
meeting people nobody invited.

## Proposal

Publication is one deliberate change, made when the conditions below hold, touching:

- the site's switch that shows the lab in its navigation and its sitemap;
- the gate in front of the lab's pages, removed; the gate in front of the workbench's
  calls to the platform stays, so reading is open and asking still needs an account;
- the site's privacy page, which gains the sentences the workbench needs (what is sent,
  what is kept, what stays in the browser);
- the front page, which gains a link to the workbench.

**The conditions.** The workbench answers and shows the arena live (RFC 0004); admission
refuses quickly rather than queueing (RFC 0001), so a crowd meets a named refusal rather
than a minute of nothing; the shield is in front of the edge (RFC 0003); every public
document passes the redaction check, which it already has to in order to build.

## Alternatives considered

**Publish each document as it is decided.** Tempting, and it is how the documents are
written: each one is public inside the lab from the day it is opened. Rejected for the
lab as a whole: a lab with a demo that cannot take a crowd is a lab that falls over in
public on the day someone shares it.

## Decision

Open. Fixed so far: one change, the four places, the conditions.
